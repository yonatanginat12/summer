Why that one is first

You have something scarce here

English is your first language. In Israel that is unusual, and parents pay for it. In April 2026 the national testing authority found that only 22% of ninth graders meet the Education Ministry's English requirements, and Israeli families already spend ₪6,000–12,000 a year on private English tutors.

Here is the part that matters. On Superprof, an Israeli tutoring site, the average tutor charges ₪108 an hour. Native English speakers on the same site charge ₪110. So being a native speaker is worth about ₪2 an hour if you compete with adult tutors.

You should not compete with them. A seven-year-old will talk to you and go quiet in front of a certified adult teacher. That is the whole business. You are not selling lessons, you are selling an hour a small child actually enjoys, and nobody else is selling it.

How the first one happens
  • Ask a parent to post once in the school parents' WhatsApp group. Coming from them it gets read; coming from you it gets scrolled past.
  • Say the age range, the day, the price per child, and that it is games and talking, not tutoring.
  • Start with two kids you already know. The first session is practice and you will be bad at it.
  • Take three or four families rather than one. Same money, and one family cancelling does not end everything.

The move that actually gets replies
  • Pick three to five businesses near you that post badly.
  • Re-edit one of their existing posts without asking. Send it and say you made it, and you can do more.
  • This works because they see the thing instead of reading a pitch, and it builds your portfolio at the same time.
  • Charge per Reel, not per hour. Nobody wants to watch a clock.

One thing about nails

Doing gel on other people is the part to be careful about

Nothing in Israel stops you legally — there is no licence or certificate needed to do someone's nails. The problem is chemical, and it lands on you rather than on the person in the chair, because you are the one doing it over and over.

People who do nail treatments have nine times the risk of a permanent allergy to HEMA, which is in most gel systems. Permanent means permanent. Once you have it, it reacts to dental materials, medical adhesives, the electrodes used for a heart trace, and the cement used in bone surgery. Getting that at 13 follows you for decades.

Gloves do not reliably stop it. In a study of 37 gel and acrylic products, the chemicals went through nitrile, neoprene and PVC gloves, and in every single product the ingredients listed on the label were not the ingredients actually in the bottle.

Also worth knowing: EU rules class HEMA products as professional-use-only, TPO was banned outright in September 2025, and dermatologists point out that nails keep developing until you are 18, so regular gel on your own nails at your age can do damage that shows up years later.

Doing your own nails occasionally is a different thing from doing five sets a week for money. Making designs on a tablet has none of this problem, which is one reason it is on the list and a nail business is not.

Money and rules

Three things that are just true

The money goes through a parent. At 13 you cannot open an Israeli bank account, and Bit, PayPal, Etsy and Fiverr all hang off one. This is not a workaround, it is how every platform that allows people your age is built. PayBox Young is the clean way for it to reach you afterwards.

You cannot be employed, but you can work for yourself. The minimum age to be someone's employee in Israel is 14, and only during official vacation. That rules out things like being a camp counsellor this year. It does not rule out any of the five above, because in all of them you are working for yourself. In June 2025 the Ministry of Labour told the Knesset it has no authority over self-employed minors at all.

Businesses need receipts. If a business pays you, they need a receipt, which a parent has to issue. Sort that out before the first payment, not after.
